Members' area

There is nothing to sign in to

Everything on this site is free and open, with no account, no sign-in and no email address. That is not a limitation — it is the whole design.

Accounts were switched off, then removed, on 2 August 2026

There used to be a login here. It asked for a password, stored a hash of it in this browser, and then unlocked a page that said there were no member-only features yet — because there weren't, and there still aren't. A password that guards nothing, never leaves your device and cannot be reset is worse than no password: it invites you to reuse a real one somewhere it can do no good.

On 2 August it was switched off. Later the same day the sign-in button, the password box and the whole members flow were taken out of the pages altogether, so there is no longer any part of this site that asks you for a password. If you ever made an account here, it only ever existed inside your own browser and nothing was ever sent anywhere. Clearing your browser data removes the last trace of it.

Nothing you could reach before is out of reach now. There was never anything behind the login.

What the login actually offered

Worth writing down plainly, because "there was a members' area" sounds like something was lost. This is the list, taken from the page's own words before it was removed — it said the same thing in three separate places:

"There are no member-only features yet — this is where they'd appear if any are built."

So: the account unlocked one page, and that page's own text said it unlocked nothing. The one thing that did exist for supporters — commissioned resources — carried a line saying signing in did not unlock it and that it was open to anyone who donates. It still is, and it still needs no account.

The machinery behind it was real enough — create an account, log in, log out, a password-strength meter, a signed-in name in the header, and a password reset that only worked with a cloud backend that was never configured. It was a working lock on a door with nothing behind it.

If you want it back

It is not a switch. There is a setting called features.accounts.enabled, and turning it on by itself changes nothing you can see — that was tested rather than assumed, by forcing the setting on and counting what appeared: no login button, no sign-in box, no password field. The sign-in markup was deleted from the pages, so bringing accounts back means restoring that markup as well as flipping the setting.

Nothing is lost and nothing needs rebuilding from scratch — it is all in the project's history. And a decision worth making again rather than by default: an account that stores a password on your device, cannot reset it, and unlocks nothing is worse than no account, because it tempts you to reuse a real password. If member-only material ever exists, the login should come back with it, not before it.
