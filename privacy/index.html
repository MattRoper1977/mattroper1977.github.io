Data and privacy

What this site sends, and what it doesn't

Written by checking the code and watching what the pages actually do, not by copying a template. Where something can't be checked from here, it says so.

There are no accounts. Nothing on this site asks you to register, and there is no password box anywhere. There used to be one; what it was and why it went is written out in full.

There are no cookies. Every page was loaded and checked: 0 of 27 pages set a cookie.

There is no analytics. No Google Analytics, no tag manager, no tracking pixel, no session recorder. Loading all 27 pages produced requests to exactly one address that isn't this site, and it is the visit counter described below.

The one thing that happens without you doing anything

Two pages — the home page and the stats page — contact counterapi.dev, a public counting service, to add one to a visit tally and read the running totals shown on screen. That's 2 pages out of 27; the other 25 contact nothing at all.

What gets sent is a request to increase a counter, and a two-letter country code. The country is worked out on your device from your browser's time-zone setting — there is no IP lookup, no location permission and no third-party geolocation service involved. Your time zone says "Europe/London", so the counter for GB goes up by one. That's the whole mechanism.

No page you visited, no identifier, and nothing you typed is sent with it.

counterapi.dev is not run by me. I can describe the request that leaves your browser, and I have; I can't make promises about what a service I don't control does with it. If that trade isn't one you want to make, blocking api.counterapi.dev costs you nothing — the counters simply show the last number your own browser saw.

About the contact form

It is the only place on this site that collects anything you type. It sits on one page, it has three fields — name, email, message — and nothing else reads them: there is no script attached to that form or to any of its fields. When you press send, your browser posts them straight to formsubmit.co and you land on a thank-you page here.

FormSubmit is a third party. I can tell you what your browser sends and where; I can't vouch for what happens at the other end. Nothing on this site can confirm a message actually reached me — the page hands it to the relay and the relay is where this site's view stops. If a reply matters, email me directly as well; it is the same inbox with nothing in between.

About the UAS Register and the ASDAN tools

These are the ones that matter most, because they hold real pupil information. Pupil names, marks, units and evidence are stored in your browser on your device and are never sent anywhere. There is no upload, no sync and no server holding a copy — which is also why backing up matters, and why the tool nags you about it.

These tools used to fetch their PDF and OCR libraries from a third-party code host the moment you used those features. Since 2 August 2026 those libraries are part of this site, so the PDF reader, the OCR engine and its English language pack all load from madebymatt.uk and nothing else. The OCR now works with no internet connection at all, which is what a tool for classrooms should have done from the start.

What stays on your device

Visiting the site leaves cached counter numbers in your browser's local storage — 37 of them, all of the form mbm_c_…, which are just the last totals your browser saw so the numbers don't flash blank on the next visit. If you change the reading background, that choice is stored too.

Opening the UAS Register or the ASDAN app creates a database in your browser (uas_register, asdan_register) to hold your work. Clearing your browser's site data deletes all of it. Nothing here has a copy.

What isn't here

  • No accounts, no sign-in, no password anywhere on the site.
  • No cookies — measured across every page.
  • No analytics or tracking service of any kind.
  • No advertising, and nothing sold or shared with anyone.
  • No newsletter and no mailing list. Nothing collects your email address except the contact form, where you type it yourself.

Links that leave the site

Links to Ko-fi and YouTube are ordinary links: nothing is contacted until you click one, and then you are on their site under their terms, not mine.

What I can't tell you

Three honest gaps, stated rather than papered over.

What the third parties do. counterapi.dev, formsubmit.co and YouTube each receive a request from your browser. I've described every one. What they keep is theirs, and any page claiming otherwise about a service it doesn't run is guessing.

Children

This is a teaching site and young people use it. That is the reason there are no accounts and nothing to sign up for: the simplest way to protect a child's data is not to collect any. The one form on the site asks an adult for a way to reply, and nothing else on any page asks anyone for anything.
